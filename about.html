About

I build electronics and the software that runs on them — mostly for hardware that has to work somewhere you cannot go and fix it.

In short

Most of my work sits on the boundary between hardware and software: the communication stack between two boards, the accelerator that has to fit in the fabric you actually have, the reliability budget that decides whether a design survives its review. That boundary is where the interesting failures live, and it is the part nobody can hand to someone else.

I am an MSc student in microelectronics at EPFL. Before that I read electrical and electronic engineering there, bilingually in English and French.

What I work on

  • Space payload electronics. At SpaceLocker I designed, built and tested payload electronics for a March 2026 orbital launch — power conditioning, data handling, watchdog and safing circuitry — and wrote flight software in C and Rust under ECSS constraints. The hardware went through TVAC, vibration and shock qualification, and the design was defended at ESA PDR and CDR.
  • FPGA and reconfigurable compute. At CERN openlab I worked on the CMS Level-1 Trigger, building the clock-domain-crossing FIFO between AXI4-Stream input and the AI Engine array on a Versal ACAP. At EPFL ESL I built core pieces of HELIOS, a CGRA toolchain for the Square Kilometre Array.
  • Rover avionics. Two years on EPFL Xplore, the last as lead system engineer for the electronics and embedded systems on the rover that won the European Rover Challenge 2025.
  • RF and analog. A year at the Laboratory of Wave Engineering on physical nonlinearities as analog neuron activations — a tunable 2.4 GHz metasurface, and an optical GELU.

How I work

  • Design the failure paths first. Timeouts, CRCs, asserts — demos shouldn't be adrenaline sports.
  • Constraints are fuel. Power, latency, EMI — the edges are what force a design to be good rather than merely different.
  • Measure, don't guess. VNAs, logic analysers, profilers. Truth lives in traces.
  • If others can't maintain it, it isn't done. Interface specs, diagrams and clear APIs are the part that survives people leaving.
