About

I build electronics and the software that runs on them — mostly for hardware that has to work somewhere you cannot go and fix it.

In short

My work sits on the boundary between hardware and software: the clock-domain crossing between programmable logic and an AI Engine array, the accelerator that has to fit in the fabric you actually have, the reliability budget that decides whether a design survives its review. That boundary is where the interesting failures live, and it is the part nobody can hand to someone else.

The throughline is reconfigurable hardware for real-time inference under constraints that do not negotiate — a fixed trigger latency, a fixed power budget, a launch date. I am an MSc student in microelectronics at EPFL, and a research assistant on a CGRA toolchain at EPFL ESL. Before that I read electrical and electronic engineering here, bilingually in English and French.

What I work on

  • FPGA and reconfigurable compute. At CERN openlab I built the programmable-logic streaming path feeding a transformer to the AI Engine array on a Versal ACAP, and measured it in hardware at 34.02 µs — within 1.25 % of simulation. At EPFL ESL I work on a CGRA toolchain: the ASTRA work targeting Square Kilometre Array signal processing, and currently the DMA engine and HELIOS.
  • Space payload electronics. At SpaceLocker I designed, built and qualified payload electronics — power conditioning, data handling, watchdog and safing — and wrote flight software in C and Rust under ECSS constraints. The hardware cleared ESA PDR and CDR and passed thermal vacuum, vibration and shock qualification.
  • Rover avionics. Two years on EPFL Xplore, the last as lead system engineer for the electronics and embedded systems on the rover that won the European Rover Challenge 2025 — first of 25 on-site teams.
  • RF and analog. A year at the Laboratory of Wave Engineering on physical nonlinearities as analog neuron activations — a tunable 2.4 GHz PIN-diode metasurface, and an optical GELU.
  • Design tooling. Three published KiCad plugins covering multi-board projects, ECSS reliability budgets and thermal analysis for vacuum — each one written because the manual workflow was costing a design cycle.

How I work

  • Design the failure paths first. Timeouts, CRCs, asserts — demos should not be adrenaline sports.
  • Constraints are fuel. Power, latency, EMI — the edges are what force a design to be good rather than merely different.
  • Measure, do not guess. VNAs, logic analysers, in-fabric probes. Truth lives in traces, and a simulation is a hypothesis until the board agrees with it.
  • Say what a result does not show. Every case study here ends with its own limits. A number you cannot bound is not a number.
  • If others cannot maintain it, it is not done. Interface specs, diagrams and clear APIs are the part that survives people leaving.
